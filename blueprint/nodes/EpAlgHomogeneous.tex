\begin{lemma}[$\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{alg}}_p$ of a homogeneous polynomial]
Let $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $p\in\mathbb{R}$, let $m\ge2$ be an integer and let
$P\in\mathbb{R}[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ be homogeneous of degree $m$. Then
$\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{alg}}_p(P)$ is homogeneous of degree $3m-4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Throughout we use the background proposition on products, scalar multiples, sums
and derivatives of homogeneous polynomials. Since $m\ge2$, all the subtractions
below are genuine.

\emph{Step 1: the derivative factors.} For every $i$, the polynomial
$\partial_iP$ is homogeneous of degree $m-1$. Applying this again, for all $i,j$
the polynomial $\partial_i(\partial_jP)$ is homogeneous of degree
$(m-1)-1=m-2$.

\emph{Step 2: the first block.} For each $i$,
$(\partial_iP)^2=(\partial_iP)(\partial_iP)$ is homogeneous of degree
$(m-1)+(m-1)=2(m-1)$. A finite sum of polynomials homogeneous of degree
$2(m-1)$ is homogeneous of degree $2(m-1)$, so
$\sum_i(\partial_iP)^2$ is homogeneous of degree $2(m-1)$. Likewise each
$\partial_i\partial_iP$ is homogeneous of degree $m-2$ by Step 1, so
$\sum_i\partial_i\partial_iP$ is homogeneous of degree $m-2$. Their product is
therefore homogeneous of degree
\[
  2(m-1)+(m-2)=2m-2+m-2=3m-4 .
\]

\emph{Step 3: the second block.} Fix $i,j$. By Step 1 the three factors
$\partial_i\partial_jP$, $\partial_iP$, $\partial_jP$ are homogeneous of degrees
$m-2$, $m-1$, $m-1$ respectively, so their product
$(\partial_i\partial_jP)(\partial_iP)(\partial_jP)$ is homogeneous of degree
\[
  (m-2)+(m-1)+(m-1)=3m-4 .
\]
Summing first over $j$ and then over $i$, and using again that a finite sum of
polynomials homogeneous of one and the same degree is homogeneous of that
degree, the double sum
$\sum_i\sum_j(\partial_i\partial_jP)(\partial_iP)(\partial_jP)$ is homogeneous of
degree $3m-4$. Multiplying by the scalar $p-2$ preserves homogeneity of degree
$3m-4$.

\emph{Step 4: conclusion.} By the definition in \noderef{EpAlg},
$\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{alg}}_p(P)$ is the sum of the polynomial of Step 2 and the
polynomial of Step 3, both homogeneous of degree $3m-4$. A sum of two
polynomials homogeneous of degree $3m-4$ is homogeneous of degree $3m-4$, which
is the assertion.
\end{proof}
