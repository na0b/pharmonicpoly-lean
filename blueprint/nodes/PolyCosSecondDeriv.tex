\begin{lemma}[Second derivative of $t\mapsto P(\cos t)$ at the origin]
Let $P\in\mathbb{R}[X]$ and define $G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by
$G(t)=P(\cos t)$. Then
\[
  G''(0)=-P'(1),
\]
where $G''$ denotes the second derivative of $G$ and $P'$ the formal derivative
of $P$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\widehat{P}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, $\widehat{P}(x)=P(x)$, for the
polynomial function attached to $P$, and likewise $\widehat{P'}$ and
$\widehat{P''}$ for those of the first and second formal derivatives of $P$. It
is classical that a polynomial function is infinitely differentiable and that
its analytic derivative is the polynomial function of its formal derivative:
$\widehat{P}\,'=\widehat{P'}$ and $\widehat{P'}\,'=\widehat{P''}$. Likewise
$\cos$ is infinitely differentiable with $\cos'=-\sin$ and $\sin'=\cos$.

\emph{Step 1: the first derivative.} We have $G=\widehat{P}\circ\cos$, a
composition of differentiable functions, so $G$ is differentiable and the chain
rule gives, for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pcsd-first}
  G'(t)=\widehat{P'}(\cos t)\cdot(-\sin t)=-\sin t\;P'(\cos t).
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: the second derivative.} The right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:pcsd-first} is the product of the differentiable function
$t\mapsto-\sin t$ and the differentiable function
$t\mapsto\widehat{P'}(\cos t)$, the latter differentiable by the chain rule
again, with derivative $t\mapsto-\sin t\,P''(\cos t)$. Hence $G'$ is
differentiable and, by the product rule, for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$,
\[
  G''(t)=(-\cos t)\,P'(\cos t)+(-\sin t)\bigl(-\sin t\,P''(\cos t)\bigr)
        =-\cos t\;P'(\cos t)+\sin^2 t\;P''(\cos t).
\]

\emph{Step 3: evaluation at $t=0$.} Since $\cos 0=1$ and $\sin 0=0$,
\[
  G''(0)=-1\cdot P'(1)+0\cdot P''(1)=-P'(1).
\]
\end{proof}
